# Scientific scope and limitations

Focused product evidence note, reviewed 2026-10-19. This is not a systematic review, a treatment recommendation, or an approved human-participant protocol.

## Product question

A visual-design tool can make external displays and optional descriptions explicit. Whether an afterimage-related experience can contribute to later voluntary mental imagery is a separate, unvalidated hypothesis. This prototype does not establish that mechanism or provide an induction/training regimen.

Keep the following categories distinct in wording and data:

1. Seeing a design that is physically displayed.
2. A residual percept after a display disappears.
3. Deliberately attempting imagery without the corresponding visible design.
4. Knowing, remembering, describing, or reconstructing its properties.
5. No visual experience, uncertainty, or a mixture.

These are product reporting distinctions, not a diagnostic classification. A reconstruction score, residual image, or higher confidence must not automatically become an imagery-improvement result.

## Two relevant primary-study examples

### Letter imagery and afterimage use

A 2019 fMRI study trained six participants to imagine letter shapes and reconstructed their imagery-related neural patterns. Participants reported using the fading stimulus's afterimage as a strategy. The study did not test restoration of voluntary imagery in an aphantasia group or isolate an afterimage intervention against an active comparison. It provides a methodological lead, not efficacy evidence for this app. Its laboratory display procedure is not adopted as consumer guidance. [Primary paper: Reconstructing imagined letters from early visual cortex](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6499877/)

### Practice and metacognitive judgments

A small 2012 imagery-practice study found improved alignment between vividness judgments and a rivalry-based measure, without an overall increase in imagery strength. Changes in reporting and changes in imagery strength can therefore be different outcomes. That particular null finding also does not prove that every possible imagery intervention is ineffective. [Primary paper: Training Visual Imagery](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00224/full)

These examples are intentionally narrow. No claim of a complete literature search, effective dose, success rate, generalization, or lasting benefit is made.

## Design consequences

- Keep the app usable without reporting an internal visual image.
- Preserve optional, neutral descriptions and absent/uncertain responses.
- Avoid streaks, forced progress, leading improvement prompts, and exposure escalation.
- Do not infer diagnosis, neural repair, or imagery gain from software outputs.
- Do not add webcam pupil tests, rivalry-derived diagnostic scores, or copyrighted questionnaires as incidental features.
- Treat usability, subjective experience, task performance, and any later efficacy endpoint separately.

## Review before broader research

A formal evaluation of imagery change needs a separately reviewed protocol: predefined outcomes, attention to expectation and practice effects, suitable comparison conditions, reference-absent transfer observations, and a plan for delayed outcomes. Timing and exposure choices require qualified review; this repository supplies no medically safe duration, intensity, or washout interval.

Human-participant recruitment, medical information collection, and external sharing require their own ethical, privacy, and authorization decisions. The software tests use synthetic fixtures only and provide no evidence about any person's experience.

Static displays and restrictive code are not a guarantee of comfort or safety. Qualified review of actual patterns, final wording, display conditions, and accessibility remains outstanding.
